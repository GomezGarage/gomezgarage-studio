Materials

Choose the material for the job

The letters describe the polymer or its reinforcement. CF means carbon fiber; GF means glass fiber. Fiber-filled filaments are generally stiffer and more dimensionally stable, but can be less impact-tough and more abrasive to print. Exact behavior depends on the filament grade and part design.

Base polymers

PLA

Polylactic Acid

Also called polylactide

Easy to print and good at holding fine detail. A practical choice for decorative objects, prototypes and parts used away from heat or heavy impact.

PETG

Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol-modified

Glycol-modified PET

A versatile option for functional parts that need more toughness and impact resistance than standard PLA, such as brackets, covers and organizers.

ABS

Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene

Named for its three component monomers

A tough engineering plastic with better heat tolerance than PLA. Often chosen for durable housings, functional fixtures and parts that may need sanding or other finishing.

ASA

Acrylonitrile Styrene Acrylate

Named for its component monomers

A durable material with good UV and weather resistance, making it a strong candidate for parts intended for outdoor use, subject to the design and conditions.

Fiber-reinforced grades

PLA-CF

Polylactic Acid + Carbon Fiber

CF = carbon fiber

Carbon fiber makes the PLA blend stiffer and helps it keep its shape. Useful for crisp-looking display pieces, lightweight fixtures and dimensional prototypes; it does not make PLA heat-proof.

ABS-CF

Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene + Carbon Fiber

CF = carbon fiber

Carbon fiber adds stiffness and dimensional stability to ABS. Useful for rigid housings, brackets and jigs where shape retention matters; the best fit depends on the load and print orientation.

ABS-GF

Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene + Glass Fiber

GF = glass fiber

Glass fiber reinforces ABS for stiffer parts with improved dimensional stability. A useful option for technical covers, fixtures and components that should resist bending.

ASA-CF

Acrylonitrile Styrene Acrylate + Carbon Fiber

CF = carbon fiber

Combines ASA's outdoor and UV resistance with the added stiffness of carbon fiber. Consider it for rigid outdoor brackets, housings and automotive accessories, after checking the part's actual exposure and loads.

ASA-GF

Acrylonitrile Styrene Acrylate + Glass Fiber

GF = glass fiber

Glass fiber adds stiffness and helps control dimensional change while ASA brings UV and weather resistance. Suited to rigid outdoor components when the chosen filament grade meets the job's needs.

PA6-CF

Polyamide 6 + Carbon Fiber

PA6 = Nylon 6 · CF = carbon fiber

A carbon-fiber-reinforced nylon for demanding functional parts that need high stiffness and strength. Commonly suited to brackets, fixtures and mechanical components; PA6 filament must be kept dry for reliable printing.

Material names describe the base polymer; CF and GF refer to short-fiber reinforcement. Filled filaments are abrasive and need a suitable hardened nozzle. Properties vary by manufacturer and grade, so we match the material to the part rather than relying on the label alone.
